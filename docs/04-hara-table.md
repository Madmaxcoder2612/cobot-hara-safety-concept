## Rationale and other measures

### H-01: Moving arm (impact)

- **Task:** Operator refills the part tray while the robot runs
- **Why S/F/P:** Without limiting, a 14 kg payload at full speed can break bones (S2). Tray refill happens several times per hour (F2). The motion is visible and the trained operator can step back (P1). Judgment call, see open point OP-1.
- **Inherently safe design:** Rounded links, no sharp edges, padded gripper
- **Information for use:** Operator training; floor marking of the collaborative workspace

### H-02: Moving arm (crushing)

- **Task:** Operator refills the part tray while the robot runs
- **Why S/F/P:** Crushing can cause irreversible injury (S2). Hands are in the tray area several times per hour (F2). Approach to the tray is slow and predictable (P1).
- **Inherently safe design:** Fixture and tray designed with gaps that avoid trapping fingers (ISO 13854) where possible
- **Information for use:** Operator training; pictogram at the tray

### H-03: Moving joints (pinching)

- **Task:** Operator works beside the arm
- **Why S/F/P:** Irreversible injury possible (S2). Operator is regularly within reach (F2). Joint motion is visible and slow near the operator (P1).
- **Inherently safe design:** Link geometry leaves clearance at joints; programmed paths avoid folded poses
- **Information for use:** Operator training

### H-04: Moving arm (impact to head)

- **Task:** Operator leans over the table to reach a part
- **Why S/F/P:** Head and face are the most sensitive body regions (S2). Leaning in is occasional (F1). The operator looks at the part and not at the arm (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** Base height and layout keep programmed paths below shoulder height
- **Information for use:** Operator training

### H-05: Sharp workpiece edge

- **Task:** Operator refills the part tray while the robot runs
- **Why S/F/P:** Cuts normally heal (S1). Hands are in the area several times per hour (F2). A thin edge is hard to see and avoid (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** Gripper holds parts with machined edges facing away from the operator; parts are deburred upstream
- **Information for use:** Cut-resistant gloves required

### H-06: Falling workpiece

- **Task:** Any task near the robot
- **Why S/F/P:** Reversible injury with safety shoes (S1). Operator is often present (F2). A falling part cannot be avoided (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** Gripper is self-locking and keeps its grip on loss of power
- **Information for use:** Safety shoes required

### H-07: Unexpected start-up

- **Task:** Operator clears a jammed part with the robot paused
- **Why S/F/P:** Irreversible injury possible (S2). Jam clearing is occasional (F1). A start from standstill comes without warning (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** -
- **Information for use:** Procedure for jam clearing; indicator lamp shows paused state

### H-08: Moving arm (impact or crushing)

- **Task:** Programmer teaches positions standing next to the arm
- **Why S/F/P:** Irreversible injury possible (S2). Teaching is occasional (F1). The programmer is very close and focused on the tool (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** -
- **Information for use:** Only trained programmers may use manual mode

### H-09: Hazardous motion continues in an emergency

- **Task:** Any task
- **Why S/F/P:** Covers situations the other functions did not foresee (S2). Emergencies are rare (F1). The endangered person may be unable to act (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** -
- **Information for use:** E-stop locations shown in the operating instructions

### H-10: Gravity (stored energy)

- **Task:** Any task under the arm
- **Why S/F/P:** Irreversible injury possible (S2). Hands are under the arm only occasionally (F1). The arm sags slowly and can be avoided (P1).
- **Inherently safe design:** Spring-applied brakes on all joints (power-off = braked)
- **Information for use:** Instruction for releasing a trapped person without power

### H-11: Live electrical parts

- **Task:** Electrician works on the open controller cabinet
- **Why S/F/P:** Electric shock can be fatal (S2). Maintenance is occasional (F1). Live parts cannot be seen (P2). Handled by electrical design and procedure, not by a control safety function.
- **Inherently safe design:** Electrical equipment per IEC 60204-1; lockable main disconnect; touch-safe terminals
- **Information for use:** Only qualified electricians; lockout-tagout procedure

### H-12: Wrong safety parameters

- **Task:** Technician changes the gripper or payload settings
- **Why S/F/P:** The limits are what keep contact harmless, so a wrong value removes the protection (S2). Changes are occasional (F1). The error is invisible to the operator (P2).
- **Inherently safe design:** -
- **Information for use:** Validation checklist to be completed after every parameter change
